Screenshot of GetResponse — captured from official site
Use GetResponse if you are a solopreneur or small-team marketer who runs straightforward email-plus-webinar campaigns and wants fast AI-assisted setup under $59/month. Skip it if you need deep conditional branching, advanced segmentation, or stable list management at scale; the builder and pricing jumps will frustrate you within months.
Strengths
  • Visual builder and AI copy tools let you launch a basic newsletter or nurture sequence in under an hour when your needs stay within simple triggers and linear flows.
  • Combines email, landing pages, popups, webinars, and funnels inside one account, removing the need to stitch separate tools for standard lead-magnet-to-sale journeys.
  • 99% deliverability rate reported by users who compare it directly to Mailchimp or ActiveCampaign on straightforward promotional sends.
    • Limitations
  • Automation builder supports only basic conditional rules and webinar triggers, forcing manual workarounds or platform switches once you need nested logic or revenue-based segmentation.
  • Contact-based pricing jumps at 1k, 2.5k, 5k thresholds, so adding 4,000 subscribers can move you from the $19 Email Marketing plan straight into the $59+ Marketing Automation tier.
  • Forms and landing pages experience random breakage that requires rebuilding or support tickets, matching the glitch reports from multiple review sites.

    Best fit / not ideal for 03
    Best fit
    Solopreneurs running weekly newsletters and simple three-email welcome sequences who benefit from the fast drag-and-drop editor and AI subject-line suggestions.
    Small ecommerce teams that host occasional webinars and need built-in funnels without adding a separate CRM or webinar platform.
    Marketers whose campaigns stay within linear automations and who value one-account simplicity over deep conditional logic.
    Not ideal for
    B2B marketers who rely on complex multi-step journeys with nested conditions, lead scoring, or dynamic revenue triggers; ActiveCampaign handles those natively.
    Teams expecting stable segmentation and list management at 5,000+ contacts; the limited filtering options and aggressive tier jumps create ongoing friction.
    Users who cannot tolerate occasional platform glitches on forms and landing pages; the reported bugs require frequent manual fixes or support intervention.
    Typical alternatives 04
    ActiveCampaign
    ActiveCampaign offers deeper conditional logic, if/then branching, and AI optimization inside its visual builder while GetResponse’s builder stops at basic rules and webinar triggers. ActiveCampaign’s automation depth suits complex B2B journeys; GetResponse bundles webinars, funnels, and landing pages in one lower-cost account.
    Choose ActiveCampaign when you run sophisticated multi-step automations with heavy branching and lead scoring. Choose GetResponse when you want an all-in-one platform with built-in webinars and fast AI website generation under $59/month.
    Brevo
    Brevo gives unlimited contacts on its free tier plus native SMS/WhatsApp while GetResponse caps free at 500 contacts and charges extra for SMS. Brevo’s starter plan starts at $25/month; GetResponse’s equivalent Email Marketing plan starts at $19/month but scales faster once contacts exceed 1,000.
    Choose Brevo when you need multichannel messaging and never want to pay per contact. Choose GetResponse when you rely on webinar hosting, conversion funnels, and AI-generated landing pages.
    HubSpot
    HubSpot ties automation directly to its CRM with contact-based triggers and lead scoring; GetResponse uses a standalone visual builder without native CRM depth. HubSpot’s advanced automation requires Professional plans starting near $890/month while GetResponse’s Marketing Automation tier begins at $59/month.
    Choose HubSpot when your entire team lives inside a CRM and you can afford enterprise pricing. Choose GetResponse when you need quick email-plus-webinar campaigns without CRM overhead.
    Inside the workflow 05
    You sign up for the Free plan, go to the dashboard, click Campaigns > Create newsletter, choose a responsive template, then use the drag-and-drop editor to add text, images, and AI-generated copy. You head to Automation > Create workflow, drag condition blocks and actions in the visual builder, set triggers like signup or webinar attendance, then activate and watch the stats tab for open and click rates. When your list grows past 1,000 contacts you repeat the process in the higher Marketing Automation tier to unlock advanced segmentation and funnels.
    • —The visual automation builder is simple enough for basic sequences but forces you to switch to clunky conditional rules when you need branching logic, exactly the limitation reported by users moving from ActiveCampaign.
    • —Pricing scales sharply at every contact threshold so adding 4,000 subscribers can jump you from the $19 Email Marketing plan straight into the $59+ Marketing Automation tier within months.
    • —Forms and landing pages occasionally break without warning, requiring you to rebuild or contact support, which matches the frequent glitch complaints in user reviews.

    Overview 07

    You run repeated campaigns that combine newsletters, simple nurture sequences, landing pages, and occasional webinars, but lose hours rebuilding flows every time your list crosses a contact threshold or you need one extra branching condition. GetResponse lets you sign up on the Free plan, open the dashboard, pick a responsive template in the drag-and-drop editor, generate copy with AI, then move to the visual Automation builder to drop triggers like signup or webinar attendance and activate the workflow. You monitor open and click rates in the stats tab and, once you pass 1,000 contacts, repeat the same process inside the higher Marketing Automation tier to unlock segmentation and funnels. Solopreneurs and small ecommerce teams who value speed of setup and an all-in-one account benefit most; they accept sharply rising prices at every contact tier and an automation builder that forces workarounds once logic exceeds basic if-then rules.

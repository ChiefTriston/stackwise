Glean is best suited for mid-market to enterprise organizations that need AI-powered search and insights across multiple data sources and platforms, particularly those using mixed technology stacks beyond just Microsoft. Organizations heavily invested in Microsoft 365 ecosystem or smaller teams may find Microsoft Copilot or other alternatives more cost-effective and simpler to implement.
Strengths
  • 100+ turnkey, permission-aware integrations across enterprise tools including Google Drive, Slack, Confluence, Jira, Zendesk, and Salesforce
  • Ecosystem-agnostic platform that builds enterprise context across all systems rather than being bound to specific applications
  • Native Microsoft connectors with Teams integration that maintains search and chat capabilities without leaving the Microsoft environment
    • Limitations
  • Custom pricing model with no transparent pricing tiers available publicly
  • No free trial or free plan available for testing the platform
  • Targets mid-market to enterprise only, making it potentially overbuilt for smaller organizations

    Typical alternatives 04
    Uare.ai
    Glean is an enterprise work AI focused on knowledge management for organizations, while Uare.ai is designed for individual users to develop and protect personalized AI that evolves over time. Both are paid solutions but serve different market segments - enterprise versus individual users.
    Choose Glean when you need enterprise-wide knowledge management and work AI capabilities for organizational use. Choose Uare.ai when you want a personalized AI assistant that develops and adapts to your individual needs over time.
    Inside the workflow 05
    You upload or receive vendor invoices into Glean AI, which automatically extracts and codes relevant data like amounts, vendors, and GL accounts. You then review the automated entries, route invoices through your configured approval workflows by department or amount thresholds, and process payments while Glean handles automated accruals and prepaid amortization schedules.
    • —Significant time savings on manual invoice entry and AP processes, but currently lacks credit card integration meaning you still need separate systems for card-based vendor spending
    • —Much cheaper than hiring dedicated AP staff and provides better functionality than basic QuickBooks, but you're dependent on a relatively newer platform without the ecosystem maturity of established AP solutions
    • —Excellent visibility and spend tracking across departments for budgeting and vendor management, but the automation means less granular control over individual transaction coding decisions
    Illustrative output 06
    Prompt
    I need to process 50 vendor invoices this month and set up approval workflows for our department heads. Can Glean help automate this and track our spending patterns?
    Output
    Glean can automatically import and code your 50 invoices, extracting key data like vendor names, amounts, and GL codes. You can set up customizable approval policies so invoices route to the right department heads based on amount thresholds or vendor types. The system will track payment status and provide spend visibility across departments for budgeting. However, credit card expenses won't be included since Glean doesn't yet support credit card program integration, so you'll need to manage those separately. Manual entry may still be needed for invoices that don't scan clearly or have unusual formats.
    Practical interpretation
    Glean provides strong accounts payable automation for traditional invoices but has gaps in credit card expense management that limit complete spend visibility.

    Overview 07

    Glean is an enterprise AI platform that combines AI agents, assistant capabilities, and search functionality to help organizations access and utilize their internal knowledge more effectively. The platform addresses the common workplace problem of information silos and inefficient knowledge discovery, where employees struggle to find relevant information scattered across multiple systems, databases, and documents within their organization. By providing AI-powered search and assistance, Glean aims to reduce time spent searching for information and improve productivity across various business functions.

    Glean works by integrating with an organization's existing data sources and systems to create a unified, searchable knowledge base powered by AI. The platform appears to offer specialized functionality for different departments and use cases, including engineering tasks like debugging code, customer support for incident resolution, sales account reviews, HR processes like new hire setup, and IT operations such as patch compliance management. The AI assistant and agents can help users find relevant information, answer questions, and potentially automate certain workflows by understanding context and providing intelligent recommendations based on the organization's data.

    Glean primarily benefits knowledge workers in medium to large enterprises who frequently need to access information across multiple systems and departments. Engineering teams, customer support representatives, sales professionals, HR staff, and IT administrators appear to be key target users based on the use cases highlighted. The main tradeoffs include the complexity and cost of implementing an enterprise-wide AI system, potential data privacy and security concerns when centralizing organizational knowledge, and the ongoing need for system maintenance and training. Organizations must also weigh the productivity gains against the investment required for integration, user adoption, and ensuring data accuracy across the platform.
